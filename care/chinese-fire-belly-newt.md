Chinese fire belly newts are cool-water amphibians that become highly aquatic once settled. Their care is built around **clean, cool water, dense aquatic planting, gentle filtration, good feeding, and avoiding heat**.

## Aquarium

A planted aquarium around **40 L / 10 gal for a small group** is a useful starting point, with larger volumes providing more stable water quality.

Provide:
- dense aquatic plants
- floating plants or resting leaves near the surface
- easy access to small land or emergent resting areas
- gentle water movement
- a secure lid

Adults are usually highly aquatic once established.

## Temperature

Cool water is essential.

A strong target range is:
- **14–20°C**
- avoid prolonged temperatures above **23°C**

Heat stress is a major husbandry risk for this species. In warm homes, cooling the room or aquarium may be more important than adding any heat source.

## Water quality

Use dechlorinated water and maintain excellent water quality with:
- a fully cycled aquarium
- gentle filtration
- regular partial water changes
- testing for ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate

Avoid strong current. These newts prefer relatively calm water.

## Feeding

Offer a varied carnivorous diet such as:
- chopped earthworms or nightcrawlers
- blackworms
- bloodworms
- daphnia
- small shrimp
- other appropriately sized aquatic invertebrates

Some individuals accept quality amphibian pellets, but whole invertebrate foods should remain an important part of the diet.

Remove uneaten food before it degrades water quality.

## Land access

Provide small, easy-to-reach resting areas above or just at the water surface.

Newly acquired or stressed animals may spend more time out of the water. This can be temporary, but persistent terrestrial behaviour can also indicate poor water quality, excessive heat, or stress.

## Group housing

Chinese fire belly newts can often be housed with members of their own species when space and food are adequate.

Avoid mixing them with fish or other amphibian species. Differences in temperature needs, competition, predation, and disease transmission make mixed-species setups a poor choice.

## Handling

Handle only when necessary. Amphibian skin is delicate and permeable.

If handling is unavoidable, use clean, wet hands or appropriate wet gloves and keep contact brief.
