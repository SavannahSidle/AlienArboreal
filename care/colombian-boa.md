Colombian boas are large, muscular, long-lived snakes that need secure housing, real thermal choice, enough space to move and climb, and a feeding plan that avoids chronic overconditioning.

## Enclosure

Adults need substantially more room than juveniles. A **4 × 2 × 2 ft** enclosure is a common minimum starting point for smaller adults, but larger animals benefit from **6 ft or more of length** and additional height for climbing.

Provide:
- a secure, escape-proof enclosure
- a snug hide on the warm side and another on the cool side
- sturdy branches or shelves that can safely support the snake
- visual cover
- a large, stable water bowl
- substrate that can hold moderate humidity without staying wet

The snake should be able to stretch out, reposition fully, and choose between warm, cool, exposed, and hidden zones.

## Temperature

Create a clear gradient rather than heating the whole enclosure evenly.

Useful targets:
- basking surface: about **32–35°C**
- warm side ambient: about **29–31°C**
- cool side: about **24–27°C**
- night: a moderate drop is acceptable, generally **20–24°C**

All heat sources should be thermostat controlled. Measure ambient temperatures with digital probes and basking surfaces with an infrared thermometer.

## Humidity & water

A practical humidity range is about **55–70%**, with temporary increases during shed if needed.

The enclosure should not remain constantly wet. Good airflow and periodic drying help prevent skin and respiratory problems.

Provide clean drinking water at all times. Many boas will soak occasionally, so the bowl should be large enough to permit it without tipping.

## Lighting

A normal day/night cycle is important. Low-level UVB is not strictly required for survival, but it can be beneficial and fits well in naturalistic setups.

Avoid bright visible lighting at night.

## Feeding

Feed appropriately sized whole prey. Prey should generally leave a modest, temporary bulge rather than massively distending the snake.

Juveniles eat more frequently than adults. As growth slows, feeding intervals should lengthen. Adult boas are especially prone to becoming overweight when fed too heavily.

Body condition matters more than rigid feeding calendars.

## Handling

Most well-established Colombian boas tolerate calm handling, but they are strong animals and should be treated accordingly.

Support the body fully, avoid sudden restraint around the head, and do not handle immediately after feeding.

Large adults should be handled with another experienced person present when appropriate.

## Growth & long-term planning

These snakes can live for decades. Plan for:
- adult enclosure size
- safe heating
- veterinary access
- transport
- feeding costs
- secure handling as the snake becomes larger and stronger
