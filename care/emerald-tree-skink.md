Emerald tree skinks are highly active, diurnal, arboreal lizards. Good care gives them **vertical space, strong lighting, UVB, a proper basking gradient, humidity with ventilation, and enough structure to climb and investigate the entire enclosure**.

## Enclosure

For one adult, use at least **60 × 45 × 90 cm (24 × 18 × 36 in)**, with larger and taller strongly preferred.

Build the enclosure vertically with:
- sturdy branches at multiple heights
- cork and climbing surfaces
- dense live or artificial foliage
- shaded retreats
- broad basking areas
- secure front-opening access

They use space aggressively, so empty vertical volume is wasted volume.

## Light & UVB

Emerald tree skinks are diurnal and should receive bright visible light plus UVB.

Provide a linear UVB lamp positioned to create a basking zone around **UVI 2–3**, while preserving shaded areas where UV exposure is much lower. Exact lamp distance depends on the fixture and mesh.

A strong daylight/grow-light bar is useful for both the animals' visual environment and live plants.

## Temperature

Provide a vertical thermal gradient:
- basking surface: around **35°C / 95°F**
- general daytime range: roughly **24–29°C / 75–85°F**
- night: approximately **20–25°C / 68–77°F**

Measure the basking surface directly and maintain cooler retreat zones.

## Humidity & hydration

A daytime average around **70%** with higher nighttime peaks works well, provided ventilation is strong.

Mist to produce drinking droplets and support humidity, but avoid stagnant air and continuously waterlogged surfaces. Automated misting can be useful in larger planted enclosures.

## Feeding

Offer a varied, appropriately sized insect diet, including:
- crickets
- roaches
- black soldier fly larvae
- silkworms, hornworms, or similar feeders as appropriate
- occasional other safe invertebrate prey

Gut-load feeders and use a balanced calcium/multivitamin schedule appropriate to the animal's UVB exposure and diet.

## Social behaviour

Emerald tree skinks can sometimes be maintained socially, but compatibility is individual and enclosure size matters. Provide multiple feeding areas, basking sites, hides, and escape routes.

Do not assume cohabitation is working simply because there are no bite wounds. Watch body condition, access to food, basking, hiding, and normal activity.

## Interaction & enrichment

Many individuals become unusually curious around keepers. Voluntary interaction is preferable to restraint. Use food, predictable routines, varied climbing routes, and enclosure changes to encourage exploration without forcing contact.
