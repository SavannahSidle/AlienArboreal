*Lygodactylus williamsi* is a very small, fast, diurnal gecko from a highly restricted range in Tanzania. It is best treated as a **display animal rather than a handling species**. Secure housing, bright light, UVB, a real thermal gradient, hydration, and appropriately tiny food matter more than enclosure decoration alone.

## Enclosure

Use a vertically oriented, escape-proof enclosure with excellent ventilation and dense usable structure. For one adult, **30 × 30 × 45 cm (12 × 12 × 18 in)** is a reasonable minimum starting point; larger is preferable, especially for a compatible pair.

Provide:
- fine branches, cork, bamboo, and broad leaves
- dense live planting and visual barriers
- multiple feeding and basking positions
- absolutely no gaps around doors, vents, cable ports, or lids

Do not house two adult males together. Watch pairs for chasing, food exclusion, weight loss, or a female being unable to retreat.

## Light & UVB

This is a strongly diurnal species. Provide:
- bright full-spectrum daylight
- a suitable **linear UVB source**
- a consistent day/night cycle, roughly 11–13 hours depending on season

UVB strength depends on fixture, mesh, distance, and basking position, so follow the lamp manufacturer's distance guidance and measure UVI where possible.

## Temperature

Create a gradient rather than one uniform temperature.

A practical target range:
- ambient daytime: **24–29°C**
- basking surface: about **31–34°C**
- night: about **20–23°C**

Use digital probe thermometers at the basking site and in a cooler shaded zone. Small enclosures overheat quickly, so heat should be controlled carefully.

## Humidity & water

Aim for moderate-to-high humidity with a daily rise and dry-down rather than constant saturation. Roughly **60–80%** is a useful working range.

Mist enough to create drinkable droplets on leaves and surfaces, then allow good airflow and partial drying between mistings. Stagnant, permanently wet conditions are not the goal.

## Feeding

Offer a varied diet of very small prey:
- fruit flies
- bean beetles
- pinhead or very small crickets
- other appropriately sized cultured insects

A small amount of quality gecko diet or fruit-based nectar diet may also be accepted.

Dust live prey with an appropriate calcium/supplement schedule matched to the UVB setup. Females producing eggs have especially high calcium demands.

## Handling

Routine handling is not recommended. These geckos are extremely quick, delicate, and capable of slipping through tiny openings. Work inside a closed room when servicing the enclosure, and use cups or enclosure-to-container transfers rather than grabbing when movement is unavoidable.

## Conservation & provenance

This species is **Critically Endangered in the wild** and internationally protected. Keep documented, captive-bred animals with clear provenance, and verify current import/export or transfer requirements before moving animals across borders.
